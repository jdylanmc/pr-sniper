# Intent: eli5

Eli5 exists to explain one subject three times over, each time for a more
expert listener: a five-year-old, a junior in the subject's own field, and an
expert who wants the details the simpler versions had to leave out.

It is the skill to run when someone hands you a name — a repository, a codebase,
a file, a system, or a plain concept — and wants to understand it quickly and at
increasing depth. It serves two people at once: the operator learning something
new, and the agent that is about to work on that something and needs its context
warmed first.

Grounding is the honest part. When the subject is a real thing that can be read,
the skill reads it — starting with how the thing describes itself — and explains
what is actually there, not what the name suggests. It reads enough to explain
the shape of the thing and then stops; it does not recite the contents of a
repository, and it does not invent an explanation for a subject it cannot find.
When it can only speak from general knowledge, it says so.

The three levels must genuinely climb. Each one adds something the last did not,
and a deeper level is allowed to correct a simplification an earlier level made
— the simple version is a kindness, not the authority. What the skill must never
produce is one explanation wearing three headings.

It stays a read-only explainer. It does not map a domain, run a discovery loop,
interrogate an idea, write a specification, or change the thing it is
explaining. Its whole job is to make a subject understood, briefly, at three
depths.
